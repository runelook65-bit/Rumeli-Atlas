# Ottoman and Islamic cultural heritage register: Skopje–Tetovo–Prizren corridor

## Register conventions

This register includes only sites or components for which an institutional, official-inventory, UNESCO/ICOMOS, or scholarly architectural source supplies a name, historical attribution, description, or condition statement. **Extant** means the historic fabric survives, even if altered or adaptively reused. **Partially extant** means only components or lower fabric survive. **Ruined/abandoned** is used only where a source explicitly describes ruins, loss of function, neglect, or a missing roof. **Destroyed** is used only where a source records destruction, collapse, or demolition; a site is not called destroyed merely because it was difficult to locate.

Coordinates are map coordinates attached to the evidence-based locality. They were geocoded from OpenStreetMap/Nominatim or taken from the IRCICA record, rather than inferred to monument-level precision from an unsourced photograph. `B` = building-level pin (usually about 10–50 m); `Q` = quarter/city or village-centroid approximation (usually about 100–500 m); `U` = uncertain/approximate because the heritage record gives only a street or quarter. The coordinate precision label is therefore separate from the historical-status confidence.

## Evidence notes on mescids, demolished sites and historic urban ensembles

- **Mescids:** ICOMOS reports that Kosovo had 79 smaller mosques/`masdjid` in 1993, 70 in active use, and that most mosques dated from the Ottoman period. That is strong regional evidence for the category, but the cited ICOMOS extract does not name a mescid in the Skopje–Tetovo–Prizren corridor, so no anonymous mescid is invented here. [2]
- **Prizren war damage:** ICOMOS found that historic Prizren survived the 1999 war without significant damage to its monuments, apart from the League of Prizren Museum. It also warns that more than 200 Kosovo mosques elsewhere were damaged or destroyed and that reconstruction sometimes ignored historic preservation. This report therefore does **not** label any named Prizren mosque destroyed on the basis of general Kosovo statistics. [2]
- **Historic urban setting:** Skopje’s Ottoman monuments were affected by the 1555 earthquake, the 1689 Piccolomini fire, the 1910 fire and the 1963 earthquake; many survived through repeated rebuilding and adaptive reuse. This explains why the register distinguishes original fabric, reconstructed fabric and current function. [4] [6]
- **Unknown dates are retained only where the source says they are unknown:** Daut Pasha Hammam’s precise year, the original date of Prizren’s Old Stone Bridge, the founding date of Kalender Baba Tomb, and the exact patron of Shatërvan Fountain are not silently filled in with guesses.
