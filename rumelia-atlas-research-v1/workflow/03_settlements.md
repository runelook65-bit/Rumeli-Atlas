# Settlements and mappable place-names in the Skopje–Tetovo–Prizren corridor

This report records places for which the consulted evidence gives a name, date, settlement or quarter status, demographic category, or current administrative identity. Ottoman terms are kept as terms of the source: **kaza**, **nahiye/nahija**, **vilayet**, **mahalle**, **karye** (village), **mezraa/mezra'a** (cultivated field or farm without a permanent settlement), **derbend** (guarded road/pass settlement), and **cemaat** (community). A source saying *Muslim*, *Christian*, *non-Muslim*, *Serbian*, *Albanian*, *Vlach*, *Turk*, or *Bosniac* is quoted or paraphrased as that source's category. No ethnicity is inferred from Muslim status, personal names, a mosque, or a religious tax category.

**Coordinate convention.** Coordinates are included only where a current gazetteer result was available. The coordinates are OSM/Nominatim point or administrative-relation results, not Ottoman survey coordinates. `place_rank 16` results are administrative or city-level centroids; `place_rank 19` results are village points. A blank coordinate means that a defensible current match was not established from the cited sources.

## What the sources support—and what they do not

**Ottoman town evidence is strongest for Tetovo/Kalkandelen and Prizren.** The Tetovo registers distinguish the settlement's two religious sections and document its change from a village-marketplace to a city and nahija centre. The Kalkandelen study independently gives the Ottoman city/kaza label, city districts, rural village/mezraa totals, and the named derbend villages. Prizren is documented as the only urban settlement in its sancak, with a detailed 1571 mahalle classification.

**The three Kalkandelen derbend names should be treated as unresolved historical mapping candidates.** Veşlovik/Yeşlovik, Mavriyani/Mavreva, and Pavrova are not safely identified with a current village by the sources used here. A map layer may retain them as historical points with `coordinate = null` and `current_status = unresolved`, pending a defter-based map, cadastral sheet, or Ottoman-to-modern toponym concordance. Similar spelling alone is not enough for a crosswalk.

**Prizren's village-level current names are better documented than their Ottoman demographic details in the open sources used here.** The OSCE/UNMIK report gives the six pilot-village forms in 2005, while the Kosovo Agency of Statistics provides a current settlement list and long population/household series. Katić's broader finding is that most villages in the sancak existed before the Ottoman period, were continuously inhabited through the Ottoman censuses, and retained medieval names with minor changes; that broad statement is not used to assert that each named pilot village has a particular Ottoman spelling or community without a village entry. [3] [4] [6]

**Administrative level matters.** `Tetovo municipality` and `Tearce municipality` census rows are not the same unit as the town or an individual village. `Prizren municipality` totals are not transferred to Piranë, Zym, Zhur, Korishë, or Sredskë. Historic `mahalle` names in Prizren are quarters and are not counted as separate villages. `Kaza`, `nahiye`, and `vilayet` are administrative units, not ethnic groups.

**Muslim status is not ethnicity.** The Ottoman sources use Muslim/non-Muslim or Christian categories for tax and registration purposes. In Tetovo, the article expressly warns that names do not yield a secure ethnic assignment. In Prizren, Katić separately reports religious change and ethnic labels for particular mahalles and regions. In Mamuşa and the modern census tables, ethnic categories are used only where the cited census or official profile explicitly reports them.
