# Skopje–Tetovo–Prizren corridor: digitized maps and reusable GIS sources

**Scope.** The corridor is treated as the north-west Macedonia–Kosovo–south Serbia/Albania zone, with historical equivalents in the Ottoman vilayets and sanjaks. “Relevant” includes sources that cover the whole Balkans or Ottoman Europe and can therefore be clipped to the corridor, as well as sources that explicitly include Kosovo, Macedonia, railways, roads, boundaries, settlements, terrain, or heritage. The record below reports what the repository page actually states. `Not stated` means it was not present on the reviewed page; it is not an inference. No file was downloaded in this research pass, and no reuse right is inferred merely from a viewer or download button.

### What the map evidence supports

* The strongest Ottoman administrative lead is the Rumsey **1909 atlas family**. Its catalog description says the 35-map atlas covers Ottoman Europe and labels vilayet boundaries, cities/towns, roads, rivers, mountains and railways. Search its related records for Kosovo, Macedonia and adjoining vilayet sheets rather than assuming the Edirne sheet itself depicts the corridor.
* The strongest ethnographic set with explicit scale and machine-readable image routes is the Cvijić/Briesemeister **1918 Balkan map** (M2/M3), followed by the British Foreign Office/War Office portfolio (M4). These are suitable for regional comparison, not for treating ethnographic boundaries as objective modern administrative borders.
* The UT Austin files are particularly useful for claims, boundary change and the 1912–1914 military-political context because the index text explicitly discusses Skoplje, Debar and the Sar range. The page is archived and its reuse status is not stated.
* The Balkan Ethnic Maps project is a valuable discovery index for Austrian/Ottoman administrative and ethnographic originals, but it is a research website, not evidence that the displayed redraws or archive-derived images are freely reusable.

## Reuse and georeferencing cautions

1. **Historical map access is not the same as a reuse license.** The repository pages for Rumsey, PCL/UT Austin, Internet Archive and the Balkan Ethnic Maps project expose images or links but do not provide a blanket public-domain/CC license. These entries are intentionally flagged unknown. The LOC records are the clearest rights cases because LOC provides a rights advisory; the Chicago transportation collection explicitly limits the stated permission to personal or scholarly use.
2. **Georeferencing potential is an assessment only.** “High” means the catalog supplies a map frame, scale, grid/prime meridian, or an explicit georeferencer route. It does not mean a repository has already produced a validated georeferenced raster. For ethnographic and claims maps, georeferencing is best understood as aligning historical polygons/labels to a modern frame, not as recovering survey-grade positions.
3. **Modern boundary datasets do not recreate Ottoman administration.** Natural Earth, geoBoundaries, GADM and EuroGeographics are contemporary political/administrative products. Use the Ottoman atlas, Austrian/War Office sheets and the 1914 claims maps for historical boundaries, then use modern GIS only as a reference frame.
4. **Gazetteer name matching needs historical aliases.** GeoNames is current/global and Pleiades is ancient-world oriented. Combine them with OCR/transcription from the map itself and preserve the original language/script (Ottoman Turkish/Arabic, German, French, Serbian/Bulgarian, English) as separate fields.
5. **Download URLs can be versioned or volatile.** Geofabrik pages are rolling extracts and list the timestamp of the current snapshot; record the page date and checksum when actually downloading. Copernicus and UNESCO use authenticated/interactive portals. This report records access routes only and does not claim that any file was downloaded.
