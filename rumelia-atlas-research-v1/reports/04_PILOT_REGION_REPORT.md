# Demographic and Ottoman administrative evidence: Skopje–Tetovo–Prizren corridor

The corridor is treated here as the Skopje/Üsküp–Tetovo/Kalkandelen–Prizren axis, with Pristina/Priştine included only where an adjoining administrative frame is explicitly documented. A **kaza**, **nahiye/nahija**, **sancak/liva**, **vilayet**, **mahalle**, **karye** (village), **mezraa**, and **derbend** are administrative or settlement categories, not ethnic categories. Ottoman records are not modern censuses: tahrir and muhasebe registers primarily enumerate taxable households, taxpayers, land, revenues, timars and institutions; temettuat enumerates taxable wealth and households; nüfus registers are selective by sex, religion, age or military purpose; salnames are annual administrative yearbooks.

## Skopje/Üsküp: tahrir, nüfus and 2021 evidence

### Sixteenth-century neighbourhood and settlement frame

The published **TD 370, 937/1530**, is the primary edition to use for Üsküb, Kalkandelen, Kırçova and neighbouring Rumeli kazas. The associated 1542/44 and later tahrir evidence reported by Zorba shows why a single “Skopje district” polygon would be misleading: the city, kaza/nahiye, kadılık and sancak labels changed, and the attached villages changed with them. The 1530 entry should be captured from the edition by its printed page and defter folio; the already verified secondary citation gives pp. 4–5 for the Paşa Liva’s right-wing kazas, while the 1542 entry gives 216 **karye** under *nefs-i Üsküp* (Zorba, p. 24). [4] [6]

 A later neighbourhood study reports tahrir-based Skopje mahalle counts of **31 in 1455, 45 in 1468, 64 in 1529 and 67 in 1569**, with later counts of 62 in 1629, 64 in 1686 and 68 in the early 18th century. These figures are counts of named urban neighbourhoods, not population totals. The study warns that missing records, loss of Skopje şer‘iyye sicilleri, the 1689 Austrian burning and migration prevent exact placement of every historical mahalle. [4] [13]

### 1832/33 NFS.d.5624

The primary record is BOA **NFS.d.5624**, an *icmal* (summary) of detailed 1832 registers. Its Ottoman title is: “*Üsküp, Kalkandelen ve Kırçova Kazaları’nda mutavattın ehl-i İslam ve reaya ve Yahudiyân ve Kıbtîyân ve Yabancıların defteri mufassallarının icmâlidir*.” The source therefore preserves the categories **ehl-i İslam**, **reaya**, **Yahudiyân**, **Kıbtîyân** and **Yabancılar**. It is not a modern ethnic census, and the 1831/32 counting program was designed partly around military manpower and cizye administration. [4, pp. 23–25]

For **Skopje city in 1833**, Zorba reports 47 mahalles. The register gives **1,236 Muslim households and 2,692 Muslim persons** across the city. Twenty-one mahalles plus the Kaleiçi are described as Muslim-only; those entries total 523 households and 1,113 persons. The report’s category is **Müslüman/Muslim**, not Turkish, Albanian or Macedonian. [4, pp. 25–26; BOA NFS.d.5624, fol. 1]

The same city summary records **1,705 non-Muslims**. Of these, **Yahudiyân** are 144 persons in 73 households, and 165 Christians are recorded as temporary residents (*misafireten*). The source says Christians occupied 25 mahalles, including mixed Muslim–Christian mahalles and five mahalles described as exclusively non-Muslim: Yahudiyan, Şevketli, Kadı Çayırı, Yeni Mahalle and Bülbül Zaim. These are the register’s religious/community categories; “Christian” and “Jewish” must not be converted into an ethnic or language label. [4, p. 25; BOA NFS.d.5624, fol. 6]

**Kıbtî** records are kept separately. **Kıbtî Müslümanlar** total 117 households and 343 persons in Skopje city, while **Kıbtî reaya** total 24 households and 59 persons. Zorba notes that Kıbtî Muslims are not classified in the same *âlâ/evsât/ednâ* cizye bands as other non-Muslims; cizye records for the latter are 7.01% *âlâ*, 47.08% *evsât*, 25.24% *ednâ*, and 20.58% exempt or below cizye age. These are fiscal categories, not income quintiles or ethnic categories. [4, pp. 26–27; BOA NFS.d.5624, fols. 5–6]

Zorba’s city total is **4,918 persons**, distributed in the article as 56.50% Muslim, 32.35% Christian, 2.98% Jewish and 8.76% Kıbtî. The article’s denominators and treatment of Kıbtî/Muslim and temporary residents should be checked against the original folios before using the percentages as a GIS total; the raw category values above are the safer records. [4, p. 26]

The rural NFS frame is also structured, not homogeneous: villages are grouped into the **Çayırova, Karadağ, Karşıyaka and Derbend** branches, with the Kaçanik nahiye listed separately. Çayırova alone has 56 villages, 19 of which contain Muslims; the article reports that the branch total includes Muslim, Christian and Kıbtî categories. These are branch aggregates and must not be assigned to each village without the underlying detailed register. [4, pp. 24, 27]

### 2021 North Macedonia census benchmark

The official 2021 publication reports **ethnic affiliation**, **religious affiliation** and **mother tongue** in separate tables. In Table 05, “Total resident population by ethnic affiliation (main groups) and sex, by municipalities,” printed pp. 34 and 46–47, the **City of Skopje** has 526,502 residents: 309,107 Macedonians, 120,293 Albanians, 8,524 Turks and 18,498 Roma; the table continues with Serbs, Bosniaks, Vlachs, Other, undeclared, unknown and persons whose data came from administrative sources. In Table 08, printed pp. 67 and 69–70, the City of Skopje has 120,269 Albanian and 7,661 Turkish mother tongues. These are modern administrative-unit totals, not the Ottoman city’s 47 mahalles. [10, pp. 34, 67, 69]

The 2021 categories must remain separate: a person counted as Albanian by ethnic affiliation is not automatically counted as Albanian mother tongue, and neither category is a proxy for Muslim religion. The same publication’s Table 06 supplies religion separately; it should be joined by municipality and census table, never inferred from Table 05 or Table 08. [10, pp. 48–62]

## Tetovo/Kalkandelen: tahrir and modern census evidence

### 1452/53 and 1467/68 city registers

Sulimani’s study distinguishes the settlement name from the administrative name. In **1452/53**, the settlement is written as **Htetova/IhTetova** and remains a village that is also a marketplace. **Kalkandele** is used for the Tetovo vilayet, not for the city centre. The city is divided into a **Muslim section of 60 households** and a **Christian section of 146 households, six widows and 36 bachelors**; five residents from Debar vilayet are also recorded. [1, pp. 45–46; fn. 13–14]

In **1467/68**, the settlement takes the name of its administrative unit/nahija in the article’s account, and Tetovo is described as a city and administrative-military centre of Polog. It is in the hass of the nahija subaşı Süleyman Bey. The register records **41 Muslim families** and **180 Christian families, 43 widows and 16 bachelors**, with three converts noted in the comparative table. [1, pp. 45–46; fn. 17–18]

There is an internal conflict that must be preserved as two records. The study’s prose says that the 1452/53 Christian section had **159 Christian families**; its comparative table gives **146 Christian families**, six widows and 36 bachelors, and the footnoted register reference is pp. 103–105. This report does not harmonise 159 and 146. Use `Sulimani_prose_159` and `Sulimani_table_146` as separate values pending inspection of the Ottoman page and the Macedonian editorial edition. **Confidence: high that the conflict exists; high for the table value as printed; medium for the underlying count until the scan is read.** [1, pp. 45–46, 49]

The study estimates roughly **1,100–1,300 persons** in 1452/53 and **1,300–1,600** in 1467/68 by applying a five-to-six-person household assumption. These are scholarly estimates, not register totals. The author explicitly cautions that ethnic affiliation cannot be securely inferred from anthroponyms and patronyms because Albanian/Arbër, Slavic and Greek/Byzantine naming layers overlap and transcription can alter forms. Therefore no Albanian, Slavic, Turkish or Macedonian population total is assigned to either register. [1, pp. 46, 49]

### 1455–1569 Kalkandelen kaza dossier

İnbaşı’s corridor-specific study supplies a second, partly overlapping sequence. For **1455**, *Vilayet-i Kalkandelen* consists of a small city centre, 138 villages and one mezraa. The city is listed as **Cemaat-i Müslümanan** and **Cemaat-i Gebran**; the article glosses *Gebran* as non-Muslim, but the source heading is retained here. It records **248 privates** in the city. In **1468**, the city total is 280 privates. [2, pp. 21–22; BOA MAD 12, fols. 101a–103a; BOA TD 4, pp. 421–426]

The urban district sequence is **six Muslim and five non-Muslim districts in 1529**, the same arrangement in 1544, and **six Muslim and six non-Muslim districts in 1569**. İnbaşı’s table reports a rise in Muslim urban privates from 60 in 1455 to 392 in 1569 and a decline in non-Muslim urban privates from 188 to 160. These are the article’s registered-person measures, not a total population or an ethnic conversion count. [2, pp. 21–22; BOA TD 149, pp. 147–148; TD 232, pp. 162–164; TK KKA TD 190, fols. 93–98]

The rural totals must also be preserved as the source’s own categories. Villages/mezraa are **138/1 (1455), 140/4 (1468), 149/8 (1481), 154/3 (1529), 156/1 (1544), and 156/0 shown (1569)**. Rural Muslim households/registered persons rise from no Muslims recorded in 1455 to 11 in 1468, 24 in 1481, 69 in 1529, 104 in 1544 and 241 in 1569. Rural non-Muslim totals are **4,801 (1455), 6,384 (1468), 5,912 (1481), 5,491 (1529), 4,497 (1544) and 4,529 (1569)**. The article calls these Muslim and non-Muslim; it provides no defensible village-level Albanian, Slavic, Turkish or language totals in the extracted tables. [2, pp. 28–29]

Three named **derbend** records should remain unresolved historical points rather than be forced onto similar modern names: **Veşlovik/Yeşlovik** (25 *kafir* derbend guards in 1455), **Mavriyani/Mavreva** (20 *kafir* guards in 1455), and **Pavrova** (1529 derbend village with ispençe and wheat obligations). *Kafir* is the source’s religious/legal term, not an ethnicity. [2, pp. 23–24; 03_settlements dossier]

### 1832/33 official Tetovo edition

The State Archives of North Macedonia announced the 1832/33 **Tetovo kaza** edition in 2021. It describes the register as a first modern Ottoman population enumeration in the territory, unlike the 15th/16th-century fiscal surveys. The edition covers eight Tetovo mahalles, 66 upper-branch villages, eight villages in the river-gorge branch, 27 lower-branch villages and 14 **Dervend** villages. The announcement specifically says the volume concerns the **Islamic population** for military-service accounting, while Christian subjects were liable to a separate tax and exempt from military campaigns. It therefore cannot be used as a full Muslim-plus-Christian census without the parallel detailed registers. **Confidence: high** for publication scope and category purpose; **medium** for exact archival shelfmark because the announcement does not expose it. [7]

### 2021 North Macedonia census benchmark

In Table 05 of the official 2021 census, **Tetovo municipality** has 84,770 residents: 15,529 Macedonians, 60,460 Albanians, 1,746 Turks and 1,885 Roma (printed p. 46, with continuation on p. 47). **Tearce municipality** has 17,694 residents: 2,114 Macedonians, 14,704 Albanians, 382 Turks and 50 Roma (p. 46). These are the census’s explicit ethnic-affiliation categories; they do not describe the 15th-century Tetovo city sections. [10, pp. 46–47]

Table 08 gives the separate mother-tongue values. Tetovo municipality has **61,006 Albanian** and **1,917 Turkish** mother tongues; Tearce has **14,705 Albanian** and **397 Turkish** mother tongues (p. 70). The table also reports the City of Skopje separately at 120,269 Albanian and 7,661 Turkish mother tongues (p. 69). These figures are municipality or city-administrative-unit rows, not town-centre or village rows. [10, pp. 69–70]

## Prizren, Prizren sancak and the linked Pristina frame

### Fifteenth- and sixteenth-century sancak and demographic evidence

Katić describes the Prizren **sancak** as usually non-contiguous: a southern area around Prizren and north-eastern Albania with a mixed Albanian–Serb–Vlach population, and a northern area between the Lim and Ibar with a Serb–Vlach population. “Albanian,” “Serb,” and “Vlach” here are the study’s historical population descriptions; they are not interchangeable with the Muslim/Christian values in the registers. [3, pp. 113–114]

The earliest cited 1477 census records **no Muslim household in the sancak**. In the 1520s, Muslims are under 2% of the registered population across the sancak, while Prizren town and its immediate vicinity are about 30%. By the mid-sixteenth century, Prizren town reaches 45% Muslims among registered people, and the 1571 register reaches 50%. These are religious categories and registered-person percentages. They do not show that a particular ethnic group converted, nor do they provide language totals. [3, pp. 127–128; BOA TD 5m; TD 368, pp. 43–45; TD 495, pp. 37–41; 167 Numaralı Muhâsebe-i Vilâyet-i Rûm-ili Defteri, vol. II, pp. 372, 392–393, 395–400]

The surrounding **nahiye** values also vary sharply: Opolje rises from 2.3% Muslims in the 1520s to 19.5% around mid-century and 82% in 1571; around 1550 the study reports Rudina 0.8%, Gora and Domštica 2%, Radovina 3% and Paštrik 4%. By 1571, the whole sancak excluding the Albanian-has areas has about 15,000 taxpayers and somewhat fewer than 1,400 Muslims; the study gives approximately 9% for that aggregate. [3, pp. 128–129; fn. 76, 81–83]

### 1571 Prizren mahalles

By the end of the sixteenth century, Christian population remained dominant in the former suburbium below the fortress, **Podgrañe**, around Orthodox and Catholic churches. The 1571 classification names **Serbian**, **mixed Serb-Albanian** and **Albanian** mahalles. The source says there were five Serbian mahalles but enumerates four names—**Stari Pazar, Nikola Mamzić, Vasilj Radomir and Pridvorica**—so both the count and the enumerated-name record must be retained, with the count flagged as internally inconsistent. It separately names **Bogoj the Fisherman** as mixed Serb-Albanian and **Panteliya** as Albanian. [3, p. 127, fn. 71–72]

On the right bank of the Bistrica, the source classifies Christian mahalles as Albanian—**Kurila/Kuka, Sinan the Scribe, Ayas Luka/Izkućan**—and one as Serbian, **mahalle-i Çarşı/Petar Nikola**. It lists Muslim mahalles around the former Church of Bogorodica Ljeviška (Old Mosque and Ljeviša), around the Bazaar (Çarşı/Jakub Bey, Ayas Bey, Hacı Kasım, Sinan the Scribe), one in Kurila, and a Tanners’ mahalle on both sides of the river. The duplicate names **Sinan the Scribe** and **Kurila** occur in different religious/ethnic descriptions; they must not be merged into one timeless community record. [3, p. 127, fn. 73–74]

The source’s categories are intentionally left in separate dimensions:

- **Religion:** Christian, Muslim.
- **Ethnicity/community labels explicitly used for mahalles:** Serbian, Albanian, mixed Serb-Albanian.
- **Broader population descriptions in the sancak:** Albanian–Serb–Vlach and Serb–Vlach.
- **Language:** no numerical language census is supplied by this 1571 mahalle passage.

### 1845 temettuat bridge

The Pristina temettuat register is relevant because it links the northern Kosovo market sphere to the Üsküp frame, but it must not be represented as a Prizren census. BOA **ML.VRD.TMT.15465** is 18 × 50 cm and 260 pages; the usable registered material is pp. **4–246**, with pp. 1–3, 177–181 and 247–260 blank or without registered information. It records household heads, occupations, land, gardens, shops, inns, mills, income and taxes for the Muslim, non-Muslim and Kıptî household heads of the Pristina town. [5, pp. 285–286]

Temettuat is a taxable wealth enumeration. It is suitable for household, occupation, property and fiscal-inequality layers; it is not suitable for a total population count unless household-size assumptions are stated separately. The register’s headings must remain **ehl-i İslam**, **reaya** and **Kıptiyan**; no Serbian, Albanian, Turkish or Slavic totals are inferred from names, occupations or religion. [5, pp. 285–287]

### 1879–1900 salname route

The Kosova Vilayeti salname series is the principal late-Ottoman administrative source for extracting year-specific sanjak/kaza/nahiye affiliations, officials, schools, roads and population tables. The eight verified İSAM volumes are 1879, 1883, 1885, 1887, 1888, 1893, 1896 and 1900. The official Kosova Vilayeti document volume directly names the Prizren, Priştine and Üsküp sanjaks in a 1879 administrative discussion (printed pp. 38–39). The report does not invent salname population values because the individual Üsküp, Kalkandelen and Prizren pages have not been transcribed here; the scan identifier and year must accompany every future extracted row. [8] [9]

### Modern Kosovo series through 2021

The Kosovo Agency of Statistics settlement table “Population and Households by settlement in the municipality of Prizren, 1948–2024” exposes the settlement rows and years 1948, 1953, 1961, 1971, 1981, 1991, 2011 and 2024. Within the requested cutoff, 2011 is the latest listed Kosovo settlement census year in this table. It includes PRIZREN and named settlements such as Piranë, Zym, Zhur, Korishë and Sredskë. The 2024 column is outside the cutoff and should not be backcast to 2021. [12]

A separate ASK table for 2011 ethnic affiliation should be queried at the settlement or municipality level. Do not transfer Prizren municipality totals to Piranë, Zym, Zhur, Korishë or Sredskë. The modern municipal boundary is not an Ottoman kaza or sancak boundary, and the ASK settlement list is a modern administrative frame. **Confidence: high** for table coverage and settlement names; **medium** for any historical continuity of a named village unless an Ottoman defter entry is read. [12]

## Conflicts and extraction controls

1. **Tetovo 1452/53 Christian count:** retain 159 (prose) and 146 (comparative table/register citation) as separate records. Do not average or “correct” one from the other. [1, pp. 45–46, 49]
2. **Prizren Podgrañe 1571 Serbian mahalles:** retain the source’s count of five and its four enumerated names. Flag the missing fifth name or counting problem. [3, p. 127]
3. **Kalkandelen status:** retain “Vilayet-i Kalkandelen” (1455), “nahiye” (1468, 1481 and 1569) and İnbaşı’s interpretive description as a kaza affiliated to Skopje Sancağı. Early Ottoman administrative terminology is not a one-to-one match with nineteenth-century units. [2, pp. 20–21]
4. **Nüfus versus tahrir:** NFS.d.5624 and the 1832/33 Tetovo edition are nineteenth-century population-register programs with religious, military, cizye or status purposes; they are not directly comparable to fifteenth-century tax households. [4] [7]
5. **Religion versus ethnicity versus language:** Muslim, Christian, non-Muslim, Yahudi, Kıbtî, Albanian, Serbian, mixed Serb-Albanian, Vlach, Macedonian, Turkish and mother-tongue categories remain source-specific. No category is inferred from personal names, mosque/church presence, tax status or language.
6. **Modern units:** 2021 North Macedonian “City of Skopje,” “Tetovo” and “Tearce” rows are modern municipal/administrative units. They are not the Ottoman city, kaza or nahiye without a separate boundary crosswalk. ASK’s Prizren rows are modern settlements and municipality rows.
7. **Unmapped historical villages:** Veşlovik/Yeşlovik, Mavriyani/Mavreva and Pavrova remain unresolved; use null geometry until a defter index, historical map or cadastral concordance provides a defensible match.

## Recommended record fields for the atlas

For every extracted row, retain: `record_id`; `place_as_written`; `modern_crosswalk`; `date_or_register`; `administrative_level`; `parent_unit_as_written`; `source_category`; `religion`; `ethnicity_or_community`; `language_or_mother_tongue`; `households`; `persons`; `widows`; `bachelors/unmarried`; `tax_class`; `source_page_or_folio`; `archive_shelfmark`; `boundary_note`; `confidence`; and `conflict_group_id`. A row with a missing value should contain `NULL`, not a value inferred from another category.
