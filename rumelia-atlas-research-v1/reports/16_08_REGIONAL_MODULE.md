# Regional memo 08 — Kosovo beyond the Prizren pilot

**Research status:** desk-research memo; URLs checked against live pages on 2 October 2026. The memo is designed for a historical atlas/data workflow, not as a political or legal position on status. It uses the present-day geographical shorthand “Kosovo” where necessary while keeping historical administrative units, present municipalities, identity categories, and name forms separate.

## 1. Scope and operating cautions

This pass extends the Prizren pilot to the principal urban nodes and associated rural historical units of **Peja/Peć/İpek, Gjakova/Đakovica/Yakova, Mitrovica/Mitrovicë, Gjilan/Gnjilane/Gilan, Ferizaj/Uroševac, Vushtrri/Vučitrn/Vulçıtırın, and Prishtina/Priština/Priştine**, with the wider Ottoman **Kosova Vilayeti** as the historical frame.

Three distinctions should be enforced in every atlas record:

1. **Administrative geography is not identity geography.** The Ottoman Kosova Vilayeti (1877–1912) was much larger than the territory administered as Kosovo today. It included, at different dates, parts of present-day Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Albania. A vilayet/sancak/kaza boundary must not be drawn as a modern state or municipality boundary.
2. **Religion, language, ethnicity/national affiliation, and legal/census category are different variables.** Ottoman registers commonly foregrounded tax status, household, confession, or legal status; they are not direct modern ethnic censuses. “Muslim,” “Orthodox,” “Catholic,” “Albanian-speaking,” “South-Slavic-speaking,” “Roma,” “Turk,” “Serb,” “Albanian,” “Bosniak,” etc. must not be collapsed into one field or back-projected without an explicit source and date.
3. **A name is a source-form, not a sovereignty claim.** Preserve the exact spelling/script used by each source (Ottoman Turkish, Albanian, Serbian, Latin transliteration), and store a separate normalized/geographic key. Do not erase alternate forms or treat one form as historically exclusive.

## 2. Historical frame: what “Kosova Vilayeti” does and does not mean

Nathalie Clayer dates the formation of the Kosova Vilayeti to **1877**, succeeding the short-lived Vilayet of Prizren and earlier Eyalet of Üsküb arrangements. Its initial sancaks included Priştina, Prizren, Dibra, İşkodra, Niş, Şehirköy and Üsküb; its borders changed repeatedly. The capital moved from Priştina to Üsküb/Skopje in 1888. The unit’s name invoked the Kosovo Polje/Kosova historical field, while its territory remained unlike the present-day Kosovo perimeter. Clayer also stresses that the present territory was assembled later, especially in the Yugoslav period after 1945–46 and with a northern change in 1959.

The **Kosova Vilayeti salname** corpus is especially useful for late-Ottoman administrative and institutional snapshots. The University of Chicago catalogue verifies entries for **1300/1883 (def'a 2), 1311/1893, 1314/1896, and 1318/1900**. Clayer notes that the first edition (1296/1879) already included a short historical account of Kosovo, and that later editions expanded the Battle of Kosovo/Murad narrative. Salnames are official yearbooks and should be read as administrative representations, not transparent population censuses.

The Turkish State Archives’ *Osmanlı Yer Adları* guide is a useful methodological companion: it states that early entries rely on **tahrir defterleri**, while later entries rely chiefly on **state and provincial salnames** and related administrative series. It also distinguishes liva/sancak, kaza, nahiye and village/mezra levels. Use it as a place-name and administrative-status crosswalk, not as a complete village gazetteer.

### Source sequence for the Ottoman period

- **Tahrir defteri (15th–16th centuries):** cadastral/fiscal surveys listing settlements, households, taxpayers, production, and sometimes personal names. They are excellent for settlement presence, administrative affiliation, and fiscal population proxies; they are not modern headcounts and do not cover every person equally.
- **Temettuat and related 19th-century fiscal registers:** household/property/income records, often useful at town or mahalle level. The Vushtrri study below demonstrates this use for 1844.
- **Nüfus defterleri (19th century):** population registers whose coverage, enumeration rules, sex/age treatment and confessional categories must be read from the particular series. “Nüfus” in a title does not guarantee a modern-style census.
- **Vilayet/state salnames:** annual or occasional administrative yearbooks. They may provide kazas, villages, public institutions, schools, roads, revenues and population by administrative category; years and boundaries must be logged record by record.
- **Consular reports and ethnographic/diplomatic statistics:** valuable for context and triangulation, but perspective- and category-dependent. The German OAPEN edition of Austro-Hungarian consular reports covers 1870–1914 and includes the first years of Serbian administration; use it alongside Ottoman records rather than as a replacement.

## 4. Rural historical units and the settlement problem

The rural layer is essential. The 15th–18th-century administrative-system article lists nahiyes and kazas associated with the Sandzak of Vuçitërna/Vushtrri, including **Vuçitërna, Prishtina, Llapi, Morava, Novobërda, Trepça, Gollaku, Sitnica, Zvečan, Janjeva** and others. It also describes the Dukagjini/Peja/Gjakova sphere and the movement of administrative weight from Altun-İli/Junik toward Gjakova in the 17th century. These lists are period-specific; they should not be converted into one “historical regions of Kosovo” layer.

Recommended rural data model:

- `source_settlement_name` — exact spelling and script;
- `normalized_name` — controlled geographic key, never a replacement for the source form;
- `source_language/script` — Ottoman Turkish Arabic script, Albanian, Serbian, Turkish Latin, etc.; 
- `date_start`, `date_end`, and `source_date` — separate the date of the event from the date of the record;
- `historical_level` — vilayet, eyalet, sancak/liva, kaza, nahiye, mahalle, village, mezra, monastery/estate, market node;
- `parent_unit_as_recorded`; 
- `modern_municipality`, `modern_settlement`, and georeference, with confidence level;
- `population_measure` — household, adult male/taxpayer, total estimated, registered resident, etc.;
- `religion/confession`, `language`, `ethnicity/national affiliation`, `legal/status category` — separate fields with `unknown/not stated` allowed;
- `migration_event` — date, direction, trigger as described by source, and evidence type;
- `heritage_id` and source URL, where applicable;
- `notes_on_uncertainty` — boundary shift, duplicate toponym, missing folio, boycott, estimate, or disputed interpretation.

Do not force an Ottoman village onto a modern point if only a broad locality is known. Use a many-to-many concordance when a historical unit contains or later fragments into several modern settlements.

## 5. Modern ASK settlement census: use and limitations

The **Kosovo Agency of Statistics (ASK)** released settlement-level ReKos 2024 data on 7 February 2025. Its official notice states that the data cover **1,469 settlements in 38 municipalities** and provide tables for population by settlement and age group, population/households by settlement, and population by settlement and sex. ASK explicitly directs users to ASKData: choose “Regjistrimi i Popullsisë” → “Sipas Vendbanimeve” → municipality → table/year; historical settlement data are available from **1948** onward where the series exists, with Excel/CSV/PDF downloads and interactive charts.

ASK’s final-results notice (19 December 2024) gives **1,602,515 registered resident population**, 50.2% male and 49.8% female, average age 34.84, and density 146.9/km². It states that more than 99.8% of coded buildings were covered, but also records a large boycott in Leposaviq/Leposavić, Zubin Potok, Zveçan/Zvečan and Mitrovicë e Veriut/Northern Mitrovica. Therefore:

- Use ASK settlement tables as the primary modern baseline for **counts, age, sex, households and time-series comparisons**.
- Preserve `enumeration_coverage` and `boycott/under-registration` flags. A census total is not a simple measure of all residents, absent persons, diaspora, displaced persons or legal title.
- Keep the **settlement** unit distinct from the **municipality** and from the historical kaza/nahiye. A municipality may contain many rural settlements, while an Ottoman kaza may cut across several modern municipalities.
- Do not infer identity from settlement names. Use ASK’s own variable labels and metadata for any language/ethnicity/religion analysis; cross-check non-response and comparability with prior censuses.
- When comparing 1948–2024, document boundary changes, renamed/merged settlements and whether a table is resident population, de facto population, household members or another measure.

Useful ASK endpoints:

- English landing page: https://askdata.rks-gov.net/pxweb/en/ASKdata/
- Albanian landing page: https://askdata.rks-gov.net/pxweb/sq/ASKdata/
- Serbian landing page: https://askdata.rks-gov.net/pxweb/sr/ASKdata/
- Official settlement release: https://ask.rks-gov.net/Releases/Details/8471
- Official final results: https://ask.rks-gov.net/Releases/Details/8429

The UN Common Kosovo Analysis repeats the 2024 total and describes an 8.8% decline from 2011, linking the decline primarily to emigration, while explicitly flagging under-registration of Kosovo-Serb residents in the northern boycott-affected municipalities. This is a useful interpretive source, but the ASK tables remain the data source for settlement-level extraction.

## 6. Heritage database and place-based evidence

The official **Cultural Heritage Database of Kosovo (TKK)** is trilingual (Albanian/English/Serbian) and provides searchable/map interfaces for **architectural, archaeological and movable heritage**. The Ministry of Culture describes the database as a tool for registration, documentation, classification and evaluation, with entries for archaeological sites, monuments/architectural ensembles, movable objects, spiritual heritage and cultural landscapes. It organizes regional views for Peja, Mitrovica, Gjakova, Gjilan, Ferizaj, Prishtina and Prizren; the Peja regional page explicitly includes Junik, Deçan and Istog, while the Mitrovica page includes Vushtrri, Skenderaj, Zubin Potok, Zvečan and Leposaviq.

Use TKK as a **heritage-record and fieldwork index**, not as a proxy for demographic ownership or an ethnic inventory. Add the database record ID, category, date/period, exact title, municipality/settlement, coordinates and protection status. The OSCE review of cultural-heritage protection notes that the legal inventory is centralized, and that temporary/permanent protection lists and database coverage have had gaps in availability and language parity; it also distinguishes officially inventoried sites from places of worship or cemeteries that communities may regard as heritage even when not formally listed.

UNESCO’s “Medieval Monuments in Kosovo” page is a separate international reference for Dečani, the Peć Patriarchate and the Church of the Holy Virgin of Ljeviša in Prizren. It is useful for significance, chronology and international listing metadata, but should not replace TKK for municipal/settlement-level inventory.

## 7. Migrations and demographic change: neutral chronology

Migration must be recorded as dated events with direction and source, not as a timeless ethnic map. A workable chronology is:

- **Ottoman conquest and consolidation (15th–16th c.):** tahrirs show settlement, household, tax and confession/status categories; religious conversion, military service, estate formation and rural/urban movement may alter counts. These records do not by themselves prove a single “replacement” narrative.
- **18th–19th c. restructuring:** wars, border changes, administrative reform, estate and market change, and movements through the Peja–Gjakova–Prizren, Priştina–Vushtrri–Mitrovica, and Gjilan/Morava corridors. Use salnames, temettuat, nüfus, consular reports and local studies together.
- **Late Ottoman / Balkan Wars / interwar period:** state transitions and violence produced displacement and resettlement, while category systems changed from Ottoman confession/status to national/administrative classifications. The German OAPEN consular edition is a major source for 1870–1914; cite report date and consular location.
- **20th-century Yugoslav and post-1945 period:** boundary and institutional changes created new administrative units and different census practices. Compare official censuses with migration studies and municipal histories, while recording whether a source counts residence, origin, citizenship, or national self-identification.
- **1990s–1999 war and after:** displacement occurred in multiple directions and affected Albanian, Serbian, Roma, Ashkali, Egyptian, Bosniak, Turkish, Croat and other communities. Use UN/OSCE/ICRC and census/return data for specific episodes; avoid totalizing “all” or “none” statements.
- **Post-2000 and current emigration:** World Bank describes Kosovo as having large international migration flows driven by economic motives and late-1990s conflict legacies. The UN 2025 analysis links the 2011–2024 resident-population decline largely to emigration, especially among youth. Treat diaspora, absent household members, returnees, internally displaced persons and current residents as separate variables.

### Identity/name protocol

Store the following independently:

- `confession/religion` — e.g., Muslim, Orthodox, Catholic, Jewish, or the source’s own historical label;
- `language` — source-observed or self-reported language, when available;
- `ethnicity/national affiliation` — only when a source uses that category and date;
- `citizenship/legal status` — a different field from ethnicity;
- `census response/non-response` — especially important for 2024 boycott-affected areas;
- `migration status` — resident, absent, displaced, returnee, emigrant, etc.;
- `name forms` — source spelling/script, modern official forms, and alternate forms as parallel fields.

A single person, household or locality may have different recorded categories across time. The atlas should display the category and date rather than harmonizing away the difference.

## 8. Practical research plan beyond the pilot

1. **Create the source register first.** Record URL, language, archive/catalogue identifier, publication date, page/folio and whether the source is primary, edited primary, official statistical, heritage inventory, or interpretive scholarship.
2. **Build a multilingual gazetteer.** Start with the seven urban nodes and their surrounding historical units; include Albanian, Serbian, Ottoman Turkish and common English/German forms. Add alternate transliterations and script forms, but one stable geographic ID.
3. **Digitize a small, auditable sample.** For each node, capture at least one tahrir-era record, one 19th-century salname/nüfus/temettuat record, one interwar/Yugoslav census or administrative source where available, and ASK settlement series.
4. **Separate scale and measure.** Do not compare a kaza total with a city-centre household count or with a modern municipality total without an explicit aggregation/coverage note.
5. **Map uncertainty.** Use confidence levels for historical-geographic joins (exact, probable, broad/uncertain). Retain unresolved toponyms rather than assigning them to the nearest modern settlement.
6. **Triangulate migration claims.** Require at least two independent evidence types for a dated movement (e.g., census/administrative register plus consular, community, UN/OSCE or oral-history source). Label estimates, contested figures and absence of evidence.
7. **Connect heritage carefully.** Link TKK/UNESCO records to settlements and historical periods, but do not infer demographic continuity, exclusive ownership or current affiliation from a monument record.

## Bottom line

The safest extension beyond the Prizren pilot is a **concordance-first** workflow: preserve Ottoman administrative and source-name forms; join them cautiously to modern ASK settlement IDs; use salname/tahrir/nüfus/temettuat for what each series actually measures; treat heritage records as place-based inventory evidence; and encode migration, religion, language, ethnicity/national affiliation and census participation as distinct, dated variables. This will allow Peja, Gjakova, Mitrovica, Gjilan, Ferizaj, Vushtrri, Prishtina and rural historical units to be compared without implying that any one period’s boundaries or categories are timeless.
